One file per species. Same selection method as the MRS-HIT page: one representative BECK_AREF file per species (Beckman covers 0.2–3 µm, the widest overlap with the 0.25–3.5 µm target range). No ASD fallback was needed for this tier — every species here has a Beckman measurement in splib07a.
Detected Bands use a lower peak-prominence threshold (0.003 vs. 0.01 on the HIT page) because several MIT/LIT spectra are relatively flat and produced zero bands at the HIT threshold (e.g. Goethite, Orthoclase). This surfaces real but weaker features — treat MIT/LIT band counts as not directly comparable in magnitude to HIT's, since the detection sensitivity differs. Values marked * fall in the ~0.95–1.15 µm or ~1.75–1.85 µm windows, where ASD/Beckman detector-splice joins are known to introduce artifacts (per ds1035.pdf).
Formulas are idealized end-member compositions from standard mineralogical references, not derived from ds1035.pdf or the spectral data itself. Hematite and Maghemite share the same stoichiometry (Fe₂O₃) but differ in crystal structure — Maghemite is shown as γ-Fe₂O₃ to distinguish it.

Sulfate Evaporites

Gypsum, jarosite, kieserite, bassanite — the four species the abstract names explicitly under MRS-MIT. Bands from SO₄ and structural H₂O sit mainly at 1.4–2.5 µm, outside the USB4000's 0.2–1.1 µm range.

Iron Oxides

Hematite, goethite, maghemite, lepidocrocite. Fe³⁺ electronic transition bands near 0.53 and 0.85–0.9 µm are genuinely visible in Vis-NIR — the one mineral class in this abstract with real USB4000-range diagnostic signal, per Viviano-Beck et al. 2014.

Bands Inside vs. Outside USB4000 Range

Per mineral: how many of its detected bands fall inside the USB4000's 0.20–1.10 µm window versus outside it, grouped into the two MRS-MIT classes. As on the HIT page, a count alone is not a diagnosis — identification needs a specific co-occurring pattern of bands, not just band presence.

1 of 8 minerals have zero detected bands inside 0.20–1.10 µm, 4 have exactly one, and 3 have two or more — concentrated in the iron oxides, consistent with their Fe³⁺ electronic-transition bands sitting in Vis-NIR rather than SWIR.
